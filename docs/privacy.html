GigGrow Privacy Policy

Last updated 25 July 2026

GigGrow is an iPhone app for gig drivers who work more than one platform. It records what you earned, how far you drove and what you spent, and estimates the tax you should set aside.

The short version. GigGrow has no accounts, no servers of ours and no analytics. Your data is stored on your iPhone and, if you leave iCloud on, in your own private iCloud database. We cannot read either one.

Who is responsible

GigGrow is published by an independent developer. Questions about this policy or about your data: enrique.padron853@gmail.com.

What GigGrow stores

Everything below is created by you, on your device, and stays there:

  • Your first name, home state and the tax and mileage rates you set.
  • Shifts: dates, hours online, and what each gig app paid — fares, tips and promotions.
  • Which gig apps you have switched on. GigGrow never asks for your login to Uber, Lyft or any other platform, and has no connection to them.
  • Recorded drives: distance, start and end time, and whether you marked the drive business or personal.
  • Expenses, including any receipt photo you attach, and vehicle and service records.
  • Screenshots you import, kept so you can check a figure against its source.

If you record a payout method, GigGrow stores the last four digits only. It never asks for a full account number, a card number or a password.

What GigGrow does not do

  • No account. There is nothing to sign up for and no password to forget. We hold no user list, because there isn't one.
  • No servers of ours. GigGrow has no backend. Your data is never sent to us and we have no way to request it.
  • No analytics, no advertising, no trackers. No third-party SDK is embedded in the app. Nothing reports how you use it.
  • Nothing is sold or shared. There is no data to sell.

iCloud sync

If you are signed in to iCloud on your device, GigGrow syncs your data through Apple's CloudKit into the private database of your own iCloud account, so it reaches your other devices and can be restored if you replace your phone.

A private CloudKit database belongs to you. Apple's design does not give the developer of an app any access to it — we cannot read, copy, export or recover your GigGrow data, and neither can anyone else with the app. Apple's handling of data in your iCloud account is covered by Apple's Privacy Policy, not by this one.

Sync is not required. GigGrow works fully offline; turning iCloud off on your device leaves the app working with the local copy alone. Settings shows the current sync state so you always know which of the two is happening.

Location, and only if you turn it on

Automatic mileage tracking is off until you switch it on, and it exists for one reason: the IRS mileage deduction requires a contemporaneous record of business miles.

  • GigGrow reads your location only to measure distance travelled, including in the background while you drive.
  • Your route is not stored. Individual coordinates are used to add up distance and are then discarded. A saved drive contains a distance, a start time, an end time and your business/personal classification — nothing that could reconstruct where you went.
  • Location data is never transmitted to us or to any third party.
  • GPS switches on when motion sensing suggests you are driving and off when you stop, which is what keeps it from draining the battery.
  • Turning the setting off stops recording immediately. Drives already recorded stay until you delete them.

Photos and the camera

Importing an earnings screenshot or scanning a receipt uses Apple's photo picker or your camera. Text is read on the device using Apple's Vision framework. No image and no recognised text is uploaded anywhere for processing. Images you import are stored with the record they belong to, like everything else.

Children

GigGrow is a tool for working drivers and is not directed at children. It does not knowingly hold data from anyone under 13.

Deleting your data

Because GigGrow has no account, there is no account to delete — but the data is yours to remove at any time:

  • Settings → Erase all data deletes every shift, drive, expense, screenshot and vehicle record. With iCloud on, that deletion syncs to your other devices too.
  • Delete the app to remove the local copy. Note that data already synced remains in your own iCloud account and will return if you reinstall — erase from within the app first if you don't want that.
  • Settings → iCloud in iOS, then Manage Account Storage, lets you remove GigGrow's iCloud data from Apple's side directly.
  • Settings → Back up writes a complete JSON file you keep, so erasing is never the same as losing.

Legal bases and your rights (GDPR / UK GDPR)

Where this applies, the processing GigGrow performs on your device is carried out to provide the app you asked for, and location processing rests on your explicit consent, which you may withdraw at any time by switching mileage tracking off. Rights of access, portability, correction and erasure are satisfied directly in the app: the data is on your device, the backup file is your export, and erase is your deletion. We hold no copy to act on.

California residents

GigGrow collects no personal information as a business under the CCPA/CPRA, sells none, and shares none for cross-context behavioural advertising.

Changes

If a future version of GigGrow changes what it stores or where — for example by connecting to a gig platform on your behalf — that will be something you opt into explicitly, and this page and the in-app privacy screen will say so before it ships. The date at the top marks the last revision.
